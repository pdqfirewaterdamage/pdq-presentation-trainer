📖 PDQ Playbook

How to convert emergency restoration alerts into signed contracts
A field manual for Tim Rozansky and the PDQ team

Mission

When a fire, water loss, or smoke event hits a home in our service area, we have a 96-hour window to win that job. After that, the homeowner has either signed with us, signed with a competitor, or decided to handle it themselves. Our entire competitive edge — speed, the PDQ Promise, equipment on board, local roots, and 917 past customers — only matters if we get in front of them in time and earn their trust before someone else does. This playbook is how we do that.

What we have going for us: We are genuinely better than 80% of our competitors. IICRC-certified, A+ BBB rated, on site in under an hour. The PDQ Promise — homeowner pays only their deductible, we bill insurance for the rest — removes the biggest friction point in restoration sales. Equipment in the truck. 917 completed jobs in the database, many within walking distance of every new fire.

What's costing us deals: The current model burns ~2 hours per lead and only ~50% of homeowners are home on the first try. Door-folder-and-come-back is the weakest part of our motion. The fix isn't to knock harder — it's to multiply our touches and build trust before the door.

The 10 Plays
1
The "I'm Almost There" pre-call
The single highest-leverage thing we're not doing today.
▼

As soon as we decide to roll on an alert, call the homeowner from the truck before we arrive. Short, soft, helpful.

"Hey [Name], this is Tim Rozansky with PDQ Restoration here in Boonton. I heard about the situation at your place on [street]. I'm not selling anything — I'm about 30 minutes out and wanted to see if you'd be open to me swinging by, free of charge, just to walk through what's next with you and the insurance side. If now's bad, totally fine, I can come back tomorrow. What works?"

If they pick up, we've started the trust relationship in the truck. If they say "come now," we know they're home. If they say "not today," we still made contact. Even on voicemail, our name is in their head first.

Conversion lift: Reps running this play go from ~25% to 40%+ sign rate. The phone is in First Knock the moment we pursue.

2
The two-touch SMS sequence
Text has 98% open rate. Email has 22%.
▼

Touch 1 — within 30 minutes of the alert:

"Hi [Name], this is Tim from PDQ Restoration in Boonton. Saw what happened at [address]. I know this is a lot. I'm local and a real person — no robo. If you want help understanding insurance + boarding/drying, reply HELP and I'll call. If not, completely fine. Hope you're safe."

Touch 2 — 24 hours later if no reply:

"Following up — no pressure. The insurance adjuster will likely call in next 48 hrs. Two things to know before they do: (1) you don't have to use whoever they recommend, (2) the longer water/smoke sits, the more secondary damage. Happy to walk you through it free. Photo of a similar job we just finished: [link]. — Tim, PDQ"

"Reply HELP" is frictionless. Touch 2 educates — adjuster's coming call, secondary damage — we sound like an advisor. First Knock already has the auto-12-hour follow-up to support this cadence.

3
The personalized video text
~70% reply rate. 80% of competitors don't do this.
▼

Before driving, stop in the truck and record 45 seconds on the iPhone:

"Hey [Name], Tim Rozansky from PDQ. I'm in Boonton. Saw what happened at your house tonight — I'm so sorry. I'm not gonna sell you anything in this video. Just wanted you to see a human face. Here's our truck, here's our crew. We're 8 minutes from your house if you want help. Reply or call — I'm here all night. Don't sign anything with anyone until you've talked to at least two restoration companies. Stay safe."

Send via SMS with a Loom link or MMS the video directly. The "don't sign until you've talked to two" line is the most disarming sentence in this business — they hear it and think we must be the honest one. They show the video to their spouse, who agrees. We just sold two people without leaving the truck.

4
The Neighbor Card
Our unfair advantage. 917 past customers — use them.
▼

First Knock surfaces nearby past PDQ customers automatically. Before we knock, call one and ask if they'd mind being a reference. Most will say yes.

"Your neighbor at [address, 3 blocks away] had a similar water loss in 2024. They said it was okay to share their number — feel free to call them and ask how PDQ handled it."

Living, breathing, local proof beats every Google review ever written. Almost no competitor has a real database of nearby past customers organized by address. We have 917 of them.

5
The First 24 Hours Survival Guide
One-page PDF. Genuinely useful. Best long-term trust play.
▼

One-page PDF: "What To Do in the First 24 Hours After a Fire or Water Loss in New Jersey."

  • Insurance: who to call first, what NOT to say
  • Documentation: photograph every room before anyone touches anything
  • Temporary housing: when ALE (Additional Living Expense) kicks in
  • Three things to demand from the adjuster
  • The PDQ Promise — at the bottom, as a footnote, not the headline

Not a sales document. Just genuinely useful. Text it within 30 minutes. Two days later when they decide who to hire, we're "the company that helped me when no one else did."

Build cost: $100 on Fiverr if we don't write it ourselves.

6
The 5-touch cadence
Run the system. Every lead. Every time.
▼

Stop thinking "I knock or I don't." Layer the touches:

WhenChannelPurpose
T+30minSMS + videoFirst contact
T+45minPhone callConfirm home, intro
T+1.5hrDoor knockSign on the spot
T+24hrSMS + PDFEducate
T+48hrEmail + caseCredibility
T+96hrFinal SMS"Adjuster come yet?"

After T+96hr: stop. Industry average sign rate is 20% because most reps do touch 3 only. Full 5-touch reps hit 45-55%. The difference isn't talent — it's the commitment to run the system on every lead.

7
Drop the folder, leave a doorhanger + handwritten note
Nobody handwrites in 2026. That's the point.
▼

When they're not home, the pocket folder is too much for a doorstep. New package:

  • Simple door-hanger with face, name, cell, 2-3 before/after photos
  • Handwritten note (literally handwrite in the truck):
"Hey [Name], stopped by — sorry I missed you. I know this week is tough. If you have 5 minutes for me to walk through your options (no obligation, no cost), text or call me anytime. — Tim 862-XXX-XXXX"

Then immediately text: "Hey — left a note at your door. Stay safe. — Tim, PDQ"

A handwritten note in 2026 is so unusual that most recipients will at least call or text to say thanks. That call is our in.

8
Build the referral network
AlertPage is reactive. Referrals are proactive.
▼

Quarterly drop-offs (coffee, donuts, branded notepads) to:

  • Plumbers — water claims start with a plumber call
  • Public adjusters — they recommend restoration companies
  • Independent insurance agents — not captive, they have flexibility
  • Property managers
  • Police/fire department coordinators
  • Local Facebook group admins — shockingly productive in NJ

Become the "I know a guy" answer. This is the lever that takes PDQ from good local shop to the obvious choice in Morris County.

9
Stay in the customer's life for 12 months
How 917 past customers becomes 2,000 in 3 years.
▼

Most restoration companies finish and disappear. We don't. After every signed and completed job:

  • Holiday card (Thanksgiving / Christmas)
  • One-year anniversary text: "Hard to believe it's been a year. How's the [room] holding up?"
  • Soft referral ask once or twice a year

They send us their cousin's neighbor, their coworker, the guy from church whose pipe burst. We don't chase those leads — they walk in pre-trusted.

10
Emergency Response Coordinator Program (pilot)
A dedicated remote coordinator who calls every lead.
▼

Overview. Implement a remote Emergency Response Coordinator to immediately call and follow up on emergency leads that we can't get to quickly, nobody was home for, or that require additional follow-up. The coordinator's sole responsibility is to professionally contact homeowners, build trust, and schedule confirmed appointments for the PDQ sales team.

Compensation Model

ComponentAmount
Per lead called$5
Per HELD appointment$100

Estimated volume: 20–30 leads per week. Estimated annual cost: ~$35,000–$60,000.

Potential Upside. Current average job value: ~$20,000 per signed job.

Added jobsMonthlyAnnual
+2 jobs$40,000~$480,000
+4 jobs$80,000~$960,000

This does not include upside from contents, mold, asbestos, rebuild, or future referrals.

How to run the pilot. Hire one coordinator. Run a 2–3 month pilot. Track: contacts made, appointments held, sign rate on prepared vs cold leads, response speed, no-show rate, revenue generated. Pay only on held appointments — confirmed, rep arrived, homeowner there. Never pay on "appointments set" or the coordinator will fill the calendar with garbage to chase commission.

Title matters: never "appointment setter." Use Emergency Response Coordinator, Homeowner Care Coordinator, or Customer Response Specialist. Homeowners need someone who sounds like a calm helper, not a salesperson.

Don't hire five. Hire one. Prove it works. Then scale.

What we do tomorrow

Don't try all ten plays at once. Layer them in.

  • Week 1: Run Play 1 on every alert. Pre-call from the truck. Will move sign rate 5–10 points.
  • Week 2: Add Play 2 (two-touch SMS). First Knock already supports it — just commit to sending every time.
  • Week 3: Record Play 3 video once, reuse for every lead.
  • Week 4: Build Play 5 Survival Guide PDF.
  • Month 2: Run full 5-touch cadence (Play 6) on every lead.
  • Month 3+: Begin Play 8 referral rollout (plumbers first).
  • When ready to scale beyond Tim: Pilot Play 10. One coordinator. 2–3 month trial.
The mindset. Speed wins the first hour. Trust wins the next 95. The reps who consistently win this business aren't the ones who knock the hardest. They're the ones who arrive at the door already half-known to the homeowner — because they called, they texted, they sent a video, they offered help before they asked for the signature.

PDQ has the credentials, the promise, the equipment, the team, and the local history to deliver. This playbook makes sure the homeowner knows it in time to choose us.
Run the system. Trust the system. Win the work.
